# CIVL1105 - past-paper workflow tracker

**20 downloaded papers, 2013 December to 2025 December; 404 tracked question/workflow rows.** All questions were reviewed, including 241 Section A questions and 163 blocks of long-question subparts. Three catalogue records requiring lecturer contact were excluded as requested.

## How consistent are the papers?

The core concepts recur strongly, but the format and emphasis change. Papers through 2015 May use 20 multiple-choice questions plus two long questions. Most later papers use 10 short questions and three or four long questions; 2023 May has 11 multiple-choice questions. 2022 May uses short descriptions/calculations in Section A. Therefore, repeated workflows are more useful than expecting an unchanged paper template.

Concrete repetitions include first-order BOD temperature conversions (2014 December Q21(c), 2015 May Q22(b), 2015 December Q13(a), 2016 December Q12(b), 2022 December Q12(a)); the same clarifier-sizing routine (2023 December Q8 and 2024 December Q7); and the lagoon/Monod performance routine in every downloaded 2022-2025 year. Recent 2024-2025 long questions also repeat membrane-assisted resource recovery, MBR classification, oxygen-sag interpretation and power-plant air controls.

The tables below are sorted by **row count, largest first**. A row represents one original question or a block of subparts sharing a workflow. Different workflows within a long question are split; adjacent steps of the same workflow are combined. Thus a three-mark MCQ and a multi-part design block each contribute one row: use row frequency to identify repetition, and the source paper marks to decide practice time. The paper-count column helps avoid overvaluing several subparts from one session.

Current-course caveat: groundwater well hydraulics, population projections, reclamation assessment and noise occur in older papers but are not explicit topics in the current first-lecture schedule. Keep those lower-priority until current teaching confirms them. Historical AQHI/criteria-pollutant questions should be interpreted in their original paper context. The original PDFs govern wording, diagrams, units and any source typos.
